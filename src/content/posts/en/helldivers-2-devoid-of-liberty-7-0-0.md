Arrowhead has a habit of stacking its big moments, and 12 August was a clear example: **Devoid of Liberty**, update 7.0.0, shipped on the same day *Helldivers 2* landed in the PlayStation Plus Game Catalog. A very large content drop timed to the exact moment a very large number of new players get handed the game for free.

Here is what changed, and what matters.

## The level cap doubled

**150 to 300.** This is the headline for anyone who has been playing since launch, because the cap has been a running complaint for a long time — a lot of the veteran playerbase has been sitting at the ceiling with nothing to progress toward, watching XP disappear into nothing after every operation.

Doubling it does not add new abilities by itself, but it restores the drip of progression that makes the mission loop feel like it is accumulating into something. For a game whose core structure is "drop, fight, extract, repeat," that drip does a surprising amount of work.

## Two new Illuminate enemies

The Illuminate roster gets two additions, and they are designed to punish different bad habits:

**The Wretch** — fast, hunts in packs, and its weak points are on its limbs rather than its head. That last detail matters more than it sounds. Everything reflexively goes for the head in this game. The Wretch specifically rewards you for unlearning that, and it arrives in numbers so you do not get long to think about it.

**The Crusher** — armoured, club-wielding, and it **regenerates health unless it is under sustained fire**. Its weak point is the helmet. The regeneration is the interesting design choice: it makes chip damage actively worthless. Plinking at a Crusher between other targets means it simply heals back through everything you have done. You commit to it or you leave it alone, and with a squad spread across an objective that is a genuinely awkward call.

Both are enemies that punish the way a competent-but-unfocused squad plays by default. That is a good sign for how the update will hold up past the first weekend.

## The Warhammer 40,000 crossover

**Castellan's Creed**, at 1,500 Super Credits for 16 rewards. Above the usual Warbond price, which is what licensed content costs.

The contents:

- **Armour with a True Grit passive** — +30% reload speed
- **R/40-K Hot-Shot Marksman Rifle**
- **G/40-K Melta Mine**
- **P/40-K Bolt Pistol**
- **A Meltagun stratagem**

On paper this is the least surprising crossover in gaming. *Helldivers 2* has always been playing in 40K's tonal register — cheerful fascism, disposable infantry, managed democracy as a phrase that would fit comfortably in an Imperial recruitment poster. The satire only lands because the aesthetic is so close to sincere. Making the reference explicit is either the obvious move or slightly deflating, depending on how attached you are to the joke staying implicit.

Mechanically, the +30% reload passive is the piece to watch. Reload speed is quietly one of the strongest stats in a game built around sustained fire against regenerating armour — which, as of this update, is now a thing you fight.

## SEAF NPCs actually help now

Smaller change, real quality-of-life impact: **SEAF NPCs now deploy in combat to heal and assist**. They were previously closer to set dressing. Making friendly NPCs functionally useful changes how defence objectives play, particularly for squads running short.

## Worth reinstalling for?

If you stopped at level 150 because there was nothing left to climb toward, yes — that specific complaint has been addressed directly. If you stopped because the enemy variety had gone stale, the Wretch and Crusher are proper additions rather than reskins, and both demand you play differently.

If you bounced off the core loop itself, nothing here changes it. It is still drop, fight, extract, repeat. It is just that the repeat now goes considerably further, and there is a bolt pistol involved.
